Buying Guides

The Complete Guitar Buying Checklist for 2026

Before you buy, use this checklist to make sure you're making a smart purchase. These are the exact criteria our editors use during the 10+ hour bench-testing protocol.

✅ Before You Buy

  • Set a realistic budget ($200–$1,000 covers 95% of needs)
  • Decide acoustic vs electric based on your genre goals
  • Research the body shape that fits your frame
  • Check the return policy of the retailer
  • Read at least 3 reviews from different sources

🔍 What to Inspect

  • Fret ends — should be smooth, never sharp
  • String action — should be low without buzzing
  • Tuning stability — tune it, play hard, check again
  • Electronics — every control should be noise-free
  • Neck relief — check for straightness along the edge

FAQ

Frequently Asked Questions

What is the best guitar for a total beginner in 2026?

For acoustic, the Yamaha FG800 ($229) is the undisputed champion. For electric, the Yamaha Pacifica 112V ($329) offers the most versatility for a learner.

How much should I spend on my first guitar?

We recommend $200–$400 for your first instrument. Below $200, quality drops significantly. Above $400, you're paying for features beginners can't yet appreciate. The sweet spot for most beginners is around $230–$330.

Should I buy acoustic or electric guitar first?

It depends on your goals. Acoustic is better for grab-and-go convenience and building finger strength. Electric is easier on the fingers and allows silent headphone practice. See our full comparison above ↑

How often should I change guitar strings?

With modern coated strings like D'Addario XS, recreational players can go 3–5 months. Session musicians should change every 15–20 hours of play for maximum brightness and intonation accuracy.

What accessories do I absolutely need?

At minimum: a tuner, extra strings, and picks. A capo and guitar stand are highly recommended. The NERECNDENS 66-Piece Kit ($21.99) covers all the basics in one purchase.

Are online guitar courses worth it?

Absolutely. Modern courses like JustinGuitar ($19.99) and Fender Play ($89.99/yr) provide better visual angles, adjustable pacing, and structured paths than most in-person lessons.
