Squier Classic Vibe 50s vs 60s Stratocaster

The Classic Vibe '50s and '60s Strats are both brilliant budget instruments with vintage-correct vibe. The differences are mostly about fretboard wood, voicing, and era aesthetics.

Fretboard wood and feel

The '50s Strat has a maple fretboard for a brighter, snappier attack and a classic 1950s look. The '60s Strat has a rosewood-style (laurel) fretboard, which feels a touch warmer and smoother under the fingers.

Tone and pickups

Both use Squier's well-regarded Fender-designed alnico pickups, voiced slightly differently per era. The '50s tends to read a touch brighter and twangier; the '60s a little rounder, but both deliver authentic Strat sparkle.

Aesthetics

It often comes down to looks: the '50s with its maple board and vintage-tint neck, or the '60s with its darker board and period styling. Both are gorgeous for the money.

Frequently Asked Questions

What's the difference between Squier Classic Vibe 50s and 60s Strats?

Mainly the fretboard wood and era styling. The '50s has a brighter-sounding maple fretboard and 1950s aesthetics; the '60s has a warmer-feeling rosewood-style board and 1960s looks. Pickup voicings differ subtly, but both deliver authentic, vintage-style Stratocaster tone.

Does maple or rosewood fretboard sound different?

Subtly. Maple (the '50s) tends to sound a touch brighter and snappier, while rosewood-style boards (the '60s) feel and sound slightly warmer and smoother. The difference is small and easily shaped with your amp, so feel and looks matter as much as tone here.

Which Classic Vibe Strat should I choose?

Pick by feel and looks. If you like a bright, snappy attack and a maple-board vintage vibe, go '50s. If you prefer a slightly warmer feel and darker board, go '60s. Both share the same excellent build and Fender-designed pickups, so you can't go wrong.
