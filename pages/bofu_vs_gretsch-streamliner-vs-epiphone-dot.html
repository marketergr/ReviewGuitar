Gretsch G2622 Streamliner vs Epiphone Dot

Two affordable semi-hollow electrics with very different personalities. The Gretsch Streamliner and the Epiphone Dot (an ES-335-style guitar) both punch above their price — here's how to choose.

Pickups and voice

The Streamliner's Broad'Tron humbuckers give a bright, jangly, articulate voice with a touch of Gretsch twang. The Epiphone Dot's classic humbuckers deliver the warm, rounded, vintage ES-335 tone that's a staple of blues and rock.

Construction and feedback

Both use a center block to tame feedback at volume, making them gig-friendly semi-hollows. The Streamliner tends to feel a little more modern and resonant; the Dot leans toward traditional warmth and sustain.

Character and looks

The Gretsch brings unique style and a distinctive jangle that stands out; the Epiphone Dot offers the timeless, do-everything 335 silhouette and sound. It often comes down to which vibe speaks to you.

Frequently Asked Questions

Is the Gretsch Streamliner or Epiphone Dot better for blues and rock?

The Epiphone Dot's warm, classic ES-335 humbucker tone is a natural fit for blues and rock. The Gretsch Streamliner can absolutely do both too, but its brighter, janglier Broad'Tron voice gives it a more distinctive character that suits indie, rockabilly, and players who want something different.

Do both handle feedback at gig volume?

Yes — both use a center block inside the semi-hollow body to reduce feedback, so they're far more stage-friendly than a fully hollow guitar. You can play them at band volume with overdrive without the runaway squeal a true hollowbody can produce.

Which semi-hollow is more versatile?

The Epiphone Dot is the more conventional all-rounder, covering jazz, blues, rock, and pop with its classic humbucker warmth. The Streamliner is versatile too but has a more distinctive jangle, so it shines when you want character as much as flexibility.
