How to Choose P-90 Pickups

P-90s are the in-between pickup — fatter than a Strat single-coil, brighter and grittier than a humbucker. Picking the right set is mostly about fit and output, so start with how they'll mount in your guitar.

Mounting: soapbar, dogear, or humbucker-sized

The format has to match your guitar's route. Soapbar and dogear are the traditional P-90 mounts; humbucker-sized P-90s (like the Seymour Duncan Phat Cat) drop straight into a standard humbucker route with no woodwork — the easy upgrade for an HB-equipped guitar.

Output and voicing

Vintage-wound P-90s stay clear, open, and dynamic; hotter-wound versions push your amp harder and suit heavier rock. Match the output to how much grit and volume you want before the amp even breaks up.

Neck vs. bridge character

Neck P-90s are warm, round, and woody; bridge P-90s are gritty and cutting. A matched set is voiced specifically for each position, so buy the pair rather than two of the same.

Hum comes with the territory

True P-90s are single-coils, so they hum like single-coils — more than a humbucker. It's part of their raw charm, but if you play high-gain or near computer screens, consider a noiseless P-90-voiced set.

Frequently Asked Questions

What's the difference between a P-90 and a humbucker?

A P-90 is a single-coil pickup (wider than a Strat's), so it's brighter, grittier, and more dynamic — but it hums. A humbucker uses two coils to cancel hum and sounds fatter, smoother, and higher-output. Tonally, the P-90 sits right between a Strat single-coil and a humbucker.

Will P-90 pickups fit my guitar?

Only if the mounting style matches. Traditional soapbar and dogear P-90s need a P-90 route. If your guitar has humbucker routes, choose a humbucker-sized P-90 such as the Seymour Duncan Phat Cat — it drops in with no modification, which is the simplest way to get P-90 tone into an HB guitar.

Do P-90 pickups hum?

Yes. True P-90s are single-coils, so they hum — especially under high gain or near screens and lighting. It's part of their raw, vintage character. If hum is a dealbreaker for you, look at noiseless or stacked P-90-voiced sets that keep most of the tone without the buzz.

Are P-90s good for blues and punk?

Excellent for both. Their gritty midrange and dynamic response make P-90s a favourite for blues, punk, classic rock, and garage tones — raw and aggressive when you dig in, yet they clean up nicely when you roll the volume back.
