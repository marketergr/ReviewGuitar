How to Pick a Beginner Electric Guitar Kit

A starter kit gets you playing on day one — guitar, amp, and accessories in one box. The trap is paying for a pile of flashy extras wrapped around a poor guitar. Judge the kit by the instrument first.

The guitar quality comes first

Everything else in the box is replaceable; the guitar is what you'll actually learn on. A solid Strat-, Tele-, or LP-style body from a reputable brand beats a no-name guitar bundled with extra 'stuff' every time.

Amp wattage for practice

A 10–15 watt practice amp is plenty for a bedroom and often includes built-in overdrive and effects to keep practice fun. You don't need more power until you're jamming with a drummer.

What's actually in the bundle

Look for the genuinely useful items: a gig bag, a tuner (or a free tuning app), a strap, spare strings, picks, and a cable. These save you a separate shopping trip; anything beyond that is bonus, not a reason to buy.

Budget for a setup

Beginner electrics often ship with high action and sharp fret ends. A quick $40–$50 setup makes the guitar far easier to play and removes a major reason beginners give up.

Frequently Asked Questions

Are beginner electric guitar starter kits worth it?

Yes, if the guitar itself is good. A kit from a reputable brand bundles a decent guitar with a practice amp and the accessories you'd have to buy anyway, usually cheaper than buying separately. Just don't let a long list of cheap extras distract you from a weak instrument.

What size amp does a beginner need?

A 10–15 watt practice amp is ideal. It's loud enough for any bedroom, usually includes onboard distortion and effects so practice stays fun, and keeps you from over-spending. Bigger amps only become necessary when you start playing with a drummer or gigging.

Strat, Tele, or Les Paul style for a first electric?

All three are great; it comes down to sound and feel. Strat-style guitars are versatile and lightweight with bright single-coils; Tele-style are simple and rugged with a twangy bite; LP-style are heavier with thick, warm humbuckers suited to rock. Pick the one whose sound and look excites you most — motivation matters more than specs.
