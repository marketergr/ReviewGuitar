Choosing a Capo for a 12-String Guitar

A 12-string puts twice the strings and more tension under the capo, so an ordinary clamp can leave you buzzing and out of tune. Here's what to look for so every course rings clean.

Even, firm clamping pressure

Twelve strings need consistent pressure across the whole neck. A quality trigger or screw capo applies firm, even force so the thinner octave strings don't buzz or choke — the most common problem with cheap capos on a 12-string.

Spring (trigger) vs. screw-adjust

Trigger capos are fast to move one-handed; screw-adjust capos let you dial in exact tension, which helps on a high-tension 12-string. If you change positions a lot mid-song, trigger; if you want precise, buzz-free pressure, screw-adjust.

Fretboard radius match

The capo's bar should match your fretboard curve. A flat capo on a curved board (or vice versa) presses unevenly and pulls some strings sharp. Many capos suit standard acoustic radii; check if your neck is unusually flat or curved.

Build and string protection

A sturdy capo with a quality rubber sleeve protects your strings and holds calibration over time. Flimsy capos lose tension and start buzzing within months of regular use.

Frequently Asked Questions

Do 12-string guitars need a special capo?

Not a dedicated one, but they demand a good one. The extra strings and higher overall tension mean a weak or uneven capo will leave the thin octave strings buzzing or out of tune. A quality trigger or screw capo that applies firm, even pressure across the neck handles a 12-string reliably.

Will a capo pull my 12-string out of tune?

It can, if it clamps unevenly or too hard. Uneven pressure pulls some strings sharp — more noticeable on a 12-string's paired octave strings. A capo with even clamping and a radius that matches your fretboard minimises this; screw-adjust models let you set just enough tension to avoid pulling sharp.

Trigger or screw-adjust capo for a 12-string?

Trigger (spring) capos are quick to reposition one-handed and fine for most players. Screw-adjust capos let you fine-tune the exact pressure, which is helpful on a high-tension 12-string to eliminate buzz without pulling strings sharp. Choose speed (trigger) or precision (screw).
