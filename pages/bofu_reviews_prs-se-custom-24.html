Is the PRS SE Custom 24 Worth It?

The SE Custom 24 is the guitar that made many players take affordable PRS seriously — it brings the brand's signature look, feel, and versatility at a fraction of a USA model's price. Here's what you're getting.

Build and woods

A carved maple top on a mahogany body, set neck, and 24-fret rosewood fingerboard give it a premium look and a resonant, balanced tone that punches well above its price.

Versatile pickups

Its humbuckers with coil-splitting cover thick rock and metal tones as well as convincing single-coil-style sparkle, making it one of the more versatile guitars in its class — great if you play multiple styles.

Playability

The wide-thin neck profile is fast and comfortable for most hands, and PRS's quality control means clean fretwork and a stable setup out of the box more often than many rivals.

Value vs. USA PRS

You give up the USA model's nitro finish, premium hardware, and some hand-finishing, but the SE captures most of the playing experience for far less — and it holds its resale value well.

Frequently Asked Questions

Is the PRS SE Custom 24 worth the money?

For most players, yes. It offers a carved maple top, versatile coil-splitting humbuckers, a fast neck, and PRS build quality at a mid-range price. It's a genuine do-everything guitar that's gig- and recording-ready out of the box, which is why it's one of the most recommended guitars in its class.

What's the difference between the SE Custom 24 and a USA PRS Custom 24?

The USA model adds a nitrocellulose finish, premium USA-made pickups and hardware, more hand-finishing, and tighter tolerances — for a much higher price. The SE, made overseas, captures most of the feel, looks, and versatility for a fraction of the cost, which is what makes it such strong value.

Is the SE Custom 24 good for beginners?

It's a great guitar to grow into. It's very playable and versatile, so a beginner won't outgrow it, though it costs more than entry-level guitars. If your budget allows, it's an instrument you can keep for years; if money is tight, a cheaper starter is fine and you can step up to one later.
