Fender Player vs American Professional II: Worth the Jump?

The Player series (Made in Mexico) and the American Professional II (Made in USA) are both excellent, but there's a big price gap. Here's what the extra money actually buys.

Pickups and electronics

The American Pro II uses upgraded pickups (V-Mod II) and refined wiring for a more nuanced, dynamic voice. The Player's pickups are very good for the price, but the Pro II's are a clear step up in clarity and character.

Fretwork and feel

The Pro II adds rolled fingerboard edges, a comfortable 'Deep C' neck, narrow-tall frets, and a bone nut for a more broken-in, premium feel. The Player feels great too, but the Pro II's finishing details are more refined.

Hardware, finish and resale

The Pro II brings better hardware (including a more stable bridge), finer finish work, and stronger resale value. The Player series offers most of the Fender experience for far less, making it the value champion.

Frequently Asked Questions

Is the American Professional II worth $800 more than the Player?

For working players and tone-focused buyers, often yes: you get better pickups, refined fretwork and neck feel, nicer hardware, and stronger resale. For beginners and budget-minded players, the Player series delivers most of the experience for far less and is the smarter value buy.

Is a Made-in-Mexico Player Fender good quality?

Yes. The Player series is consistently well-built and gig-ready, and many pros use them happily. The American Pro II refines the details — pickups, frets, finish, hardware — but the Player is a genuine, quality Fender, not a compromise instrument.

Will I notice the difference as a beginner?

You'll feel the Pro II's smoother fret edges and slightly nicer neck, but a beginner won't fully exploit the pickup and hardware upgrades early on. Many players start with a Player and step up to an American later once their ears and hands know what they want.
