Choosing a Case for Flying With Your Guitar

Air travel is where guitars get cracked necks and crushed tops. A proper case is cheap insurance against an expensive repair. Here's what to look for if you fly with your acoustic.

Hardshell vs. ATA flight case

A standard hardshell case is fine for careful travel and gate-checking, but for checked baggage an ATA-rated molded flight case with rigid walls gives far more impact protection. The rougher the handling, the more case you want.

TSA-friendly locking latches

TSA-approved locking latches let security inspect the case without breaking your locks, while still keeping it secure in transit. It's a small feature that saves a lot of stress at the airport.

Snug, supportive fit

A case that fits your body shape (dreadnought, concert, etc.) holds the guitar firmly with neck support, so it can't shift and stress the neck joint during rough handling. A loose fit is almost as risky as no case.

Weight and humidity

Flight cases protect best but weigh more, which matters for airline limits — balance protection against weight. Tucking a humidifier inside also guards against the dry cabin and destination climate cracking your top.

Frequently Asked Questions

Can you fly with an acoustic guitar?

Yes. Many airlines let you carry a guitar on if there's room in the cabin, but it's never guaranteed, so you must be prepared to gate-check or check it. If it might go in the hold, a rigid hardshell or ATA flight case is essential — a gig bag offers almost no protection against baggage handling.

Do I need a flight case or is a hardshell enough?

For carry-on or careful gate-checking, a quality hardshell case is usually enough. If your guitar will be checked into the hold, a molded ATA flight case with rigid walls and locking latches is far safer — checked baggage takes serious abuse that a standard hardshell may not survive.

Should I loosen the strings before flying?

Slightly detuning (a step or two) is a common precaution — it reduces tension on the neck in case of temperature and pressure changes or impact. You don't need to slacken them completely; just take a little tension off, and add a case humidifier to protect against dry cargo holds.
