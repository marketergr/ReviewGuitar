Martin D-28 vs Taylor 814ce: Tradition vs Modern

Two professional flagships with very different philosophies. The Martin D-28 is the classic dreadnought benchmark; the Taylor 814ce is the modern, versatile grand auditorium. Here's how to choose.

Body shape and balance

The D-28 is a dreadnought — big, bold, and bass-forward for powerful strumming. The 814ce is a grand auditorium: more balanced and articulate across the strings, which suits fingerstyle and mixed playing better.

Tone character

Both use rosewood back and sides, but the D-28 leans warm, woody, and traditional, while Taylor's voicing and bracing give the 814ce a clearer, more modern, piano-like articulation that records beautifully.

Playability and electronics

Taylor necks are famously slim and easy, and the 814ce includes Taylor's Expression System electronics for plugging in. The D-28 is a purely acoustic classic with a more traditional neck feel — built to be mic'd.

Frequently Asked Questions

Is the Martin D-28 or Taylor 814ce better for fingerstyle?

The Taylor 814ce generally suits fingerstyle better. Its grand auditorium body and Taylor's articulate voicing give excellent note separation and balance, while the D-28's dreadnought body is bigger and more bass-forward — superb for strumming but less defined for intricate picking.

Which one is better for recording?

Both record well, but the 814ce's clarity, balance, and built-in electronics make it the more plug-and-play studio guitar. The D-28 offers a classic, warm dreadnought tone that engineers love when mic'd, but it needs more careful mic placement to tame its low end.

Do they feel different to play?

Yes. Taylor necks tend to be slimmer and feel fast and modern out of the box, while Martin necks have a more traditional profile. If easy playability is a priority, the 814ce usually wins; if you want a classic dreadnought feel, the D-28 delivers it.
